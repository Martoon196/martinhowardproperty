# Property terms, in plain English

This glossary explains how the terms are used in this book. A particular contract, finance product or legal rule may use a more specific definition.

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Appraisal | Your assessment of a deal: value, income, costs, funding, risks and expected result. |
| Article 4 direction | A local planning measure that removes specified permitted development rights in a defined area. Check the actual direction and proposal. |
| Bridging finance | Short-term borrowing used to fund a purchase or another stage before repayment through a defined exit. |
| BRR / BRRR | Buy, refurbish, refinance; the extra R is often used for rent. Check what a particular speaker means. |
| Building regulations | Standards covering relevant building work. Building-control approval is separate from planning permission. |
| Capital appreciation | An increase in a property's value over time. A valuation increase is not cash received. |
| Cash flow | Money coming in and going out over a period. State which costs, reserves, loan payments and taxes the calculation includes. |
| Comparable / comp | A similar property or transaction used as evidence for value or rent. The differences matter. |
| Completion | The point at which a property transaction completes under its contract. Completing a sale and completing building work are different events. |
| Contingency | An allowance for uncertainty beyond the work and costs already budgeted. |
| Conveyancer | The legal professional handling the property transaction and relevant title enquiries. |
| Covenant | An obligation or restriction affecting land or property. Ask how the particular wording affects your plan. |
| Creeping freehold | A description sometimes used for the part of one freehold property extending beneath another ownership. |
| Drawdown / tranche | A release of loan money. A works facility may release funds in stages against specified evidence. |
| Due diligence | The investigations and checks you carry out before making a commitment. |
| Equity | The value of your interest after deducting debt secured against the property, before relevant selling costs and tax. |
| Exit | The route to the next stage of a deal, such as selling or replacing short-term finance with a longer-term loan. |
| Flying freehold | Part of a freehold property extending over another ownership. Rights, maintenance arrangements and lender requirements need checking. |
| Freehold | Ownership of the land and property interest without a lease term counting down. Rights, obligations and restrictions can still apply. |
| GDV: gross development value | The estimated total value of the completed development before deducting project costs. |
| Gross yield | Annual rent divided by the stated price or cost, multiplied by 100, before deductions. Identify the denominator. |
| Heads of terms | A summary of proposed deal terms. Have your adviser establish which provisions, if any, are binding. |
| HMO: house in multiple occupation | Broadly, accommodation occupied by people from more than one household who share facilities. The applicable legal definitions and requirements need checking. |
| Holding costs | Costs incurred while owning the property, including relevant finance, insurance, utilities and other running costs. |
| Interest-only | Loan payments that cover interest without routinely repaying the principal. The principal still needs a repayment route. |
| Leasehold | Ownership of a property interest for the remaining term of a lease, subject to its terms. |
| Loan-to-value / LTV | The loan expressed as a percentage of the value used by the lender. It does not establish the net cash available. |
| Monitoring surveyor | A surveyor who checks specified project matters for a lender, often including progress for staged funding. Their role may differ from your project manager's. |
| Net proceeds | Money remaining after stated deductions. Check exactly which deductions are included. |
| Occupancy rate | The proportion of available units or nights occupied or booked. Check the measurement period and definition of availability. |
| Permitted development / PD | Planning rights allowing specified work or changes of use when the relevant conditions and limits are met. Some routes require prior approval. |
| Practical completion | A contractual construction milestone. Its meaning depends on the agreement and is distinct from a sale completion. |
| Prior approval | A process required by certain permitted development rights for specified matters to be considered before proceeding. |
| Rebridge | Replacing bridging finance with another short-term facility. Availability, fees and repayment requirements need assessing. |
| Refinance | Replacing existing borrowing with a new loan. |
| Retained interest | Interest held back from a facility under its terms, which can reduce the initial net funds available. |
| Scope of works | The description of what will be done, to what standard, with responsibilities and exclusions made clear. |
| Service charge | A charge payable under relevant arrangements towards services, maintenance or other building costs. Check the documents and anticipated expenditure. |
| Serviced accommodation / SA | Accommodation operated for short stays, with associated guest services and operating costs. |
| Snagging | Identifying defects or unfinished items and arranging for them to be put right. |
| Title | The legal ownership interest and associated rights and obligations recorded in the relevant documents. |
| Variation | An agreed change to the works or contract. Record its price, time effect and approval. |
| Void | A period when a rental property or room is empty and does not produce the expected rent. |
