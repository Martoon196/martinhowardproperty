# Chapter 1
## Does This Fit Me?

You can buy a property at a good price and still buy yourself a job you hate.

I know because that is how we started.

Our first property venture was a serviced apartment in Cardiff. My business partner, Meirion, and I set it up beautifully. The place looked the part. The bookings were another matter.

We had started at a difficult time of year. Some weeks, Friday arrived with the weekend still empty. Then a booking might come in, help us break even, and leave us watching the calendar again.

Between bookings came the cleaning, guest questions and all the little details. Coffee sachets. Fresh bedding. No hair left on a pillow. One guest who was supposed to have a quiet stay held a party and upset the neighbours.

We had spent time thinking about the apartment. We needed to spend more time thinking about the business we would run inside it.

That distinction could save you a lot of money.

A lovely property does not automatically make a lovely business. A big return on a spreadsheet does not tell you how your week will feel. And a deal that suits someone with a full-time team may be a terrible fit for someone with a job, children and two spare evenings.

I want you to learn that before you buy, while changing your mind costs you very little.

### What are you trying to achieve?

Before you search for a property, write down what you want it to do for you.

Perhaps you want extra monthly income. Perhaps you want to build a pot of money by buying, improving and selling. Perhaps you want to own assets for the next twenty years.

Each goal changes what you should look for. It changes how long your money can stay tied up, how much work you can take on and which risks you can afford.

“Make money from property” is too loose to guide a purchase.

“Build towards £500 a month after property costs, while keeping my job” gives you something you can assess.

Now a proposed deal has to answer a useful question: will this move me towards that goal, with the money and time I have?

You can change your strategy later. You need a clear starting point today.

Try finishing this sentence: **“Over the next ___, I want property to give me ___, and I can commit ___ without putting my household under pressure.”**

If you cannot finish it, that is your first piece of work. An agent cannot choose your goal for you. A course cannot make a deal fit your life. You have to set the brief.

### Can you afford the whole deal?

People often start with the deposit. They see enough money in the bank to cover it and assume they are ready.

Try this simple example. These are made-up figures to show the problem.

You have £30,000 available after protecting your household reserve. The deposit is £25,000. That leaves £5,000.

Then you allow £4,000 for purchase costs and £8,000 for work.

| Item | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Deposit | £25,000 |
| Purchase costs | £4,000 |
| Planned work | £8,000 |
| Total so far | £37,000 |
| Your available deal money | £30,000 |
| Shortfall | £7,000 |

You are already short before allowing for a delay or an unexpected repair. The property may look affordable when you look at the deposit. The full plan says otherwise.

We will build proper appraisals later. For now, separate your money into three amounts: what you need to keep for your life, what you can commit to the deal, and what you can hold for problems.

Do not count the same pound twice. Money reserved for a roof repair cannot also be the money that pays your household bills.

### What job comes with the property?

When someone shows you a return, ask what work sits behind it.

With a single let, somebody must manage the tenancy, arrange repairs and watch the costs. With a flip, somebody must control the works and get the property sold. With serviced accommodation, somebody must run the bookings, cleaning and guest service.

You can pay people to do that work. Put their cost into the numbers, and decide who will check it is being done.

In Cardiff, the ongoing operation quickly became difficult for us. We moved into finding properties for investors instead. We researched opportunities, assessed them, marketed them and built relationships through online property communities.

That work suited us better. We had found a role we wanted to do.

Before choosing your own approach, picture an ordinary Tuesday in that business. Then picture a Friday evening when something goes wrong.

Who gets the call? Who can attend? Who makes the decision?

If the answer is you, make sure the return is worth the responsibility.

### The ordinary Tuesday test

Imagine two people looking at the same tired rental. One has spare weekday time, a trusted contractor and cash left after completion. The other works long hours, lives two hours away and needs rent to start immediately.

The building has not changed. The suitability of the deal has.

Before a viewing, write down the first three jobs the purchase would create. For a refurbishment, that might mean agreeing the scope, checking prices and arranging access. For a ready-to-let property, it might mean checking the condition, arranging management and establishing what must happen before a tenant moves in.

Now give each job a name, a cost and a place in the diary. If all three are blank, you have found a gap in your plan. Fix it while the property still belongs to someone else.

This is how you start turning “I could do that” into “this is how I would do it”.

### What can you take on today?

Ambition is useful. It can also make a complicated project look like a shortcut.

A conversion might offer more potential profit than a straightforward rental. It might also need specialist advice, more cash, more decisions and a longer wait before income starts.

You do not have to work through every strategy in order. You do need to understand the demands of the particular deal in front of you.

| Approach | A question to answer before going further |
|---|---|
| Single let | Does the rent leave enough after realistic costs and allowances? |
| Flip | Can I fund the work and hold the property while waiting for a sale? |
| HMO | Can I meet the requirements and run shared accommodation properly? |
| Serviced accommodation | Is there demand at a price that covers the full operation? |
| Conversion | Can the proposed use be approved, funded and built? |

If you need help, name the person or professional role. Find out what they will do, what they will charge and what remains your responsibility.

“We will get someone in” leaves an important part of the plan unanswered.

### How much uncertainty can you carry?

Suppose the work takes three months longer. Could you keep paying the bills?

Suppose the rent comes in below your estimate. Would ownership still be manageable?

Suppose the refinance releases less money than you hoped. Could you leave more cash in the property?

These questions help you choose a project you can stay in control of. Later, we will put numbers against them.

For now, be honest about your position. A delay that is manageable for one buyer can force another into a rushed sale.

Your first deal should leave you able to make the next decision calmly.

Here is a useful distinction: a problem can be fixable and still be wrong for you. A specialist may know exactly how to solve it, but the cost, delay or cash required may fall outside your limits.

“Someone else would buy it” is no reason for you to buy it.

### Three ways to lose your own argument

**Chasing the biggest headline.** A high advertised yield deserves questions. What costs sit underneath it? Is the rent supported by evidence? Who does the work? We will pull those figures apart in Chapter Five.

**Making the plan depend on everything going right.** If you need the work finished on the earliest date, the highest rent achieved and every pound released on refinance, you have very little room to manoeuvre.

**Changing your rules because you like the house.** You set a maximum budget, then find a property with “so much potential”. Suddenly the budget becomes flexible, the contingency gets smaller and the awkward questions can apparently wait.

Write your limits before you fall in love with a listing. That gives you something firmer to refer to when enthusiasm starts doing the maths.

### Your first buying brief

Write your answers on one page:

- **My main goal:** what I want property to do for me.
- **My starting approach:** the strategy I will investigate first.
- **My available cash:** after keeping a household reserve.
- **My time:** hours I can genuinely give each week.
- **My team:** the help I need to arrange and cost.
- **My limit:** the sort of setback I could cope with.
- **My rejection rule:** the condition that would make me leave a deal alone.

If two answers clash, fix that before you start offering. A major refurbishment and no weekday availability, for example, means you need a credible management arrangement and the budget to pay for it.

### Before you turn the page

Complete your brief. Then check whether its answers agree with one another.

If you want income soon but your chosen strategy needs months of building work, there is a mismatch. If the deal uses every pound you have, where will the first surprise be paid from? If you have no time to manage it, whose fee belongs in the budget?

You do not need a perfect plan. You need one honest enough to guide the next step.

You now know what you are trying to buy and what you can take on. The next question sounds easy: **where should you look?**

But two houses can sit in the same postcode, look similar online and serve completely different markets. Search too broadly and you waste evenings. Choose a street on the strength of a cheap asking price and you could spend years discovering why it was cheap.
